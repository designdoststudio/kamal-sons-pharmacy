How to Read Medicine Labels and Understand Expiry Dates

Health Information Guide | Kamal Sons Pharmacy

Medicine packaging contains important information that helps patients and caregivers identify a product and understand how it should be used and stored.

Before using a medicine, take a moment to check its name, active ingredient, strength, instructions, warnings and expiry date. Understanding these details can help reduce common medicine-use mistakes.

1. Check the Medicine Name

Most medicines have a brand name printed prominently on the box or bottle. The active ingredient, also known as the generic name, may be printed nearby or in smaller text.

The active ingredient is the substance responsible for the medicine's intended effect.

  • Look for the full medicine name.
  • Check the active ingredient, if listed.
  • Do not identify a medicine only by its colour or shape.
  • Ask a pharmacist if the name or ingredient is unclear.

2. Understand Medicine Strength

Strength tells you how much active ingredient is present in a particular dosage unit or amount of medicine.

Common examples of strength units include:

  • mg – milligrams
  • mcg – micrograms
  • mg/ml – milligrams per millilitre
  • % – percentage concentration
Important: Two products with similar brand names may have different strengths or formulations. Always check the exact product prescribed or recommended by your healthcare professional.

3. Read the Dosage and Directions

The directions explain how a medicine should be used. Depending on the product, they may include the amount, timing, route of administration and other instructions.

Look for information such as:

  • How much medicine is prescribed or recommended.
  • How often it should be used.
  • Whether it is taken with or without food.
  • Whether it is for oral, topical or another route of use.
  • How long the treatment should continue.
Safety reminder: Never guess a dose from the packaging alone. Follow your doctor's prescription or the product's approved instructions. Ask a pharmacist if the directions are unclear.

4. Understand Warnings and Precautions

Medicine labels and patient information leaflets may contain warnings that are important for safe use.

Examples include:

  • Possible allergies or sensitivity warnings.
  • Warnings about pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  • Possible interactions with other medicines.
  • Age-related precautions.
  • Warnings about driving or operating machinery.
  • Instructions such as shake well, do not crush or use only as directed.

Read the warnings carefully. If you have an existing health condition or take other medicines, discuss the product with a qualified healthcare professional.

5. How to Find the Manufacturing and Expiry Dates

The manufacturing date and expiry date are commonly printed on the outer carton, bottle, tube or blister packaging.

  • MFG: Manufacturing date, when the product was made.
  • EXP: Expiry date, the date after which the product should not be used.
  • Batch No. / LOT No.: A code identifying a particular production batch.

The position of these details can vary between manufacturers and products. Check all sides of the packaging and the individual container where applicable.

6. How to Understand an Expiry Date

Expiry dates may be printed in different formats depending on the manufacturer.

For example:

  • EXP 02/2027
  • EXP FEB 2027
  • EXP 15-02-2027

If a date is printed as a month and year, do not guess how the manufacturer intends it to be interpreted. Follow the packaging or leaflet instructions, and ask a pharmacist if the format is unclear.

Never use an expired medicine. Do not assume that a medicine is safe or effective after its expiry date, even if its appearance has not changed.

7. What Is a Batch Number?

A batch number, sometimes called a lot number, identifies a specific production batch of a medicine.

Manufacturers and regulatory authorities can use this information to trace products if a quality issue or product recall occurs.

If you report a suspected quality problem, keep the packaging and note the batch number and expiry date where possible.

8. Check Storage Instructions

Storage instructions explain the conditions in which a medicine should be kept.

Common instructions may include:

  • Store below a specified temperature.
  • Protect from direct sunlight.
  • Protect from moisture.
  • Keep the container tightly closed.
  • Refrigerate at the specified temperature.
  • Do not freeze.

Not every medicine needs refrigeration. Always follow the instructions for the particular product.

For more information, read our guide: How to Store Medicines Safely at Home.

9. Check the Packaging Before Use

Before using a medicine, inspect its packaging and container.

  • Check whether the outer packaging is damaged.
  • Look for signs of leakage or broken seals.
  • Check whether the printed details are readable.
  • Do not use a product if you suspect tampering.
  • Ask a pharmacist if the medicine looks unusual or different.

10. What If the Label Is Missing or Unclear?

Never take an unidentified medicine based only on its appearance or someone else's description.

If the label is damaged, missing or difficult to read, show the medicine and its packaging to a pharmacist or contact the manufacturer for guidance.

Quick Checklist Before Using a Medicine

  • Correct medicine name and active ingredient.
  • Correct strength and dosage form.
  • Clear instructions for use.
  • Warnings and precautions checked.
  • Expiry date checked.
  • Packaging appears intact.
  • Storage instructions understood.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does EXP mean on a medicine box?

EXP means expiry date. Do not use a medicine after its stated expiry date.

What does MFG mean?

MFG means manufacturing date, which indicates when the product was manufactured.

What is the difference between a brand name and a generic name?

A brand name is the product name selected by its manufacturer. A generic name identifies its active ingredient.

Can I use a medicine if its expiry date is unreadable?

Do not guess the expiry date. Ask a pharmacist or the manufacturer to help identify the product and verify its details before use.

Can two medicines with similar names have different strengths?

Yes. Similar brand names may have different strengths, ingredients or dosage forms. Always check the complete product details.

Final Words

Reading medicine labels is a simple but important habit. Checking the medicine name, strength, instructions, warnings, batch number and expiry date can help you make more informed decisions about medicine safety.

If any information is unclear, consult a qualified pharmacist or healthcare professional rather than guessing.
