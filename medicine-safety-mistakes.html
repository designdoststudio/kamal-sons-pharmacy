Common Medicine Safety Mistakes to Avoid

Medicines can help manage health conditions when used correctly. However, using medicines incorrectly may cause unwanted side effects, medication errors or serious harm.

Many common medicine-related problems can be prevented by reading labels, following professional instructions and storing medicines safely. This guide explains everyday mistakes and simple ways to avoid them.

Important: Always follow the instructions provided by your doctor, pharmacist or the medicine label. If anything is unclear, ask a qualified healthcare professional before using the medicine.

1. Taking Medicine Without Reading the Label

One common mistake is taking medicine from memory without checking its name, strength, directions or expiry date.

Before using a medicine:

  • Check the medicine name and strength.
  • Read the instructions carefully.
  • Check how and when it should be used.
  • Review warnings and storage instructions.
  • Check the expiry date and packaging condition.

2. Taking Someone Else's Medicine

A medicine prescribed for one person may not be suitable for another person, even if their symptoms appear similar.

  • Never use another person's prescription medicine.
  • Do not share your prescribed medicines with family members or friends.
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist about suitable treatment for your own situation.

3. Taking More Medicine Than Directed

Taking extra medicine does not necessarily make recovery faster. It may increase the risk of harmful effects or accidental overdose.

  • Follow the prescribed or labelled directions.
  • Do not increase the amount yourself.
  • Do not take an extra dose because you think the previous dose was ineffective.
  • If you miss a dose, check the medicine instructions or ask a pharmacist what to do.
Remember: Never double a missed dose unless a qualified healthcare professional or the medicine instructions specifically tell you to do so.

4. Using Two Medicines With the Same Active Ingredient

Some different brands and combination products may contain the same active ingredient. Taking them together without checking can result in accidentally taking too much of that ingredient.

For example, some pain, cold and flu products may contain overlapping ingredients.

  • Check the active ingredients on every medicine label.
  • Do not assume different brand names mean different ingredients.
  • Ask a pharmacist to check for duplicate ingredients before combining products.

5. Ignoring Medicine Interactions

Some medicines may interact with other medicines, vitamins, herbal products, supplements, foods or drinks.

Always tell your doctor or pharmacist about everything you use, including prescription medicines, over-the-counter products and herbal supplements.

Ask whether any food, drink or other medicine should be avoided while using your treatment.

6. Stopping or Changing Prescribed Treatment Yourself

Stopping a prescribed medicine, changing its amount or changing how often you use it without professional advice may affect your treatment.

  • Follow the treatment instructions you were given.
  • Do not change or stop long-term medicines without discussing it with your healthcare professional.
  • If you experience side effects, seek professional advice promptly.
  • Follow the prescribed duration of treatment.

7. Using Household Spoons for Liquid Medicines

Kitchen teaspoons and tablespoons are not reliable measuring devices. They can hold different amounts of liquid.

For liquid medicines, use the measuring syringe, cup or device supplied with the product. If you do not have the correct measuring device, ask your pharmacist.

For children, carefully check the product label and use the correct measuring device. Never guess a child's dose.

8. Crushing or Breaking Tablets Without Checking

Not every tablet or capsule can safely be crushed, split, chewed or opened. Some medicines have special coatings or release mechanisms.

Before crushing or splitting a medicine, ask your pharmacist or doctor whether it is suitable. Never assume that every tablet can be crushed.

9. Storing Medicines in Unsafe Places

Heat, moisture and unsuitable storage conditions may affect some medicines.

  • Follow the storage instructions on the packaging.
  • Keep medicines in their original labelled containers whenever possible.
  • Do not store medicines in damp bathrooms or near heat sources unless specifically instructed.
  • Refrigerate only medicines that require refrigeration.
  • Keep all medicines away from children and pets.

For more information, read our guide: How to Store Medicines Safely at Home.

10. Ignoring Expiry Dates or Changes in Medicine

Do not use expired medicines. Also avoid using medicines that appear damaged or have unusual changes in colour, smell, texture or appearance.

  • Check expiry dates regularly.
  • Do not use damaged or suspicious products.
  • Ask a pharmacist how to dispose of unwanted or expired medicines safely.
  • Do not use a medicine if you are unsure about its condition.

Learn more in our guide: How to Read Medicine Labels and Understand Expiry Dates.

11. Leaving Medicines Within Children's Reach

Children may mistake tablets, vitamins or liquid medicines for sweets or drinks. Even a small accidental exposure can be dangerous.

  • Keep medicines out of children's sight and reach.
  • Use a locked cabinet or secure storage container.
  • Close safety caps properly after every use.
  • Put medicines away immediately after using them.
  • Never describe medicine as candy.
Emergency advice: If a child or another person may have swallowed medicine accidentally, seek urgent medical help immediately. Do not wait for symptoms and do not try to make the person vomit unless a medical professional tells you to.

12. Not Keeping a List of Medicines

People who use several medicines may find it difficult to remember every product and its instructions.

Keep an updated list containing:

  • Names of prescription medicines.
  • Over-the-counter medicines.
  • Vitamins and supplements.
  • Known medicine allergies.
  • Important instructions provided by healthcare professionals.

Share this list with your doctor or pharmacist, especially when starting a new medicine.

Simple Medicine Safety Checklist

  • Read the medicine label before use.
  • Check the name, strength and expiry date.
  • Follow the prescribed or labelled directions.
  • Never share prescription medicines.
  • Check for duplicate active ingredients.
  • Tell your healthcare professional about other medicines and supplements.
  • Use the correct measuring device for liquid medicines.
  • Store medicines according to their instructions.
  • Keep medicines safely away from children.
  • Ask a pharmacist if you are unsure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take two different brands of the same medicine together?

Not without checking their active ingredients. Different brands may contain the same ingredient. Ask a pharmacist before combining them.

Should I take an extra dose if I forget my medicine?

Do not automatically take an extra or double dose. Instructions depend on the medicine. Check the patient information or ask your pharmacist.

Can I use expired medicine?

Do not use expired medicine. Ask a pharmacist about safe disposal and replacement.

Can I give my medicine to a family member with similar symptoms?

No. A medicine suitable for one person may be unsafe for another. They should seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

What should I do if I am unsure how to use a medicine?

Do not guess. Read the label and contact your pharmacist or doctor for clear instructions before using it.

Conclusion

Safe medicine use starts with small but important habits: reading labels, following directions, checking active ingredients, storing medicines properly and asking questions when something is unclear.

Taking these precautions can help reduce preventable medicine-related mistakes and protect your family.
